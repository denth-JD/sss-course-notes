## Part C: Case questions (50 marks, 25 each)

**C1. Tipper overturn at bridge embankment**

At 4.30 pm after heavy rain, a tipper lorry reversed to the edge of a bridge approach embankment to tip fill. The edge gave way and the lorry rolled down the slope. The driver suffered a fractured arm and was hospitalised, then given **14 days' medical leave**. No banksman was present. The main contractor had subcontracted earthworks to a local company.

1. Is this accident reportable to DOSH? Explain how and when it must be reported. (5 marks)
2. Identify **four** possible causes, covering both unsafe acts and unsafe conditions. (8 marks)
3. Describe the steps of the accident investigation the SSS should carry out. (6 marks)
4. Recommend controls to prevent recurrence, and state who is legally responsible, citing OSHA. (6 marks)

**C2. HIRARC for lifting and pitching precast piles**

A piling rig is lifting 12 m precast RC piles from a stack and pitching them into the leader for driving. The site is on soft ground, 10 m from a river.

1. Identify **four** hazards in this activity, each with its possible consequence. (8 marks)
2. Rate each hazard using the DOSH 2008 L × S matrix. (6 marks)
3. Propose controls for each hazard following the hierarchy of control, and show the residual risk. (8 marks)
4. State the legal basis for carrying out this HIRARC. (3 marks)

## Model answers: Part B

**B1. Employer's general duties, s.15(2)** (any five, "so far as is practicable")

- (a) Provide and maintain plant and systems of work that are safe and without risks to health
- (b) Arrangements for safe use, operation, handling, storage and transport of plant and substances
- (c) Information, instruction, training and supervision
- (d) Maintain the place of work in a safe condition, with safe access and exit
- (e) Safe working environment with adequate welfare facilities
- (f) Develop and implement procedures for emergencies (new, 2022)

**B2. Hazard vs risk**

- **Hazard** = a source or situation with potential to cause harm.
- **Risk** = the combination of how likely the harm is and how severe it would be (L × S).
- Example: the soft, unconsolidated edge of the embankment is the **hazard**. The **risk** is the chance a tipper overturns there × the severity (driver death). Rated L3 × S4 = 12, Medium.

**B3. Improvement vs prohibition notice** (both issued by a DOSH officer under s.48)

| | Improvement notice | Prohibition notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When | A contravention of OSHA or its regulations exists | An activity involves a risk of serious bodily injury |
| Effect | Work may continue; remedy within the stated period | Activity must stop until the risk is remedied |
| Site example | Scaffold inspection records not kept | Piling rig on an unstable platform |
| If ignored | Offence under s.49 | Offence under s.49 |
| Challenge | Appeal under s.50 (verify period) | Appeal under s.50; the notice stays in force meanwhile (verify) |

**B4. Key changes in the 2022 amendment** (any five)

- Covers all places of work (except armed forces and ships)
- s.18A: duties of principals toward contractors and subcontractors
- s.18B: mandatory risk assessment
- s.26A: employee's right to remove themselves from imminent danger
- s.29A: OSH Coordinator for 5 or more employees
- s.15(2)(f): emergency procedures
- s.31A: mandatory training for specified employees
- Penalties raised, e.g. s.19 up to RM500,000
- FMA 1967 repealed; plant now regulated under OSHA

**B5. Piling hammer noise, hierarchy of control**

| Level | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Elimination | Choose a non-impact method, e.g. jacked-in or bored piles, at design stage |
| Substitution | Quieter hammer or a low-noise hydraulic hammer model |
| Engineering | Hammer shroud or acoustic enclosure; noise barriers; well-maintained pile cushion |
| Administrative | Noise risk assessment; restricted working hours; job rotation; signage at hearing protection zone; audiometric testing |
| PPE | Earmuffs or earplugs with enough attenuation; double protection near the rig |

**B6. Duties of a Site Safety Supervisor** (any six)

- Inspect the site daily and record unsafe acts and conditions
- Run safety induction and toolbox talks
- Take part in HIRARC and check that controls are in place
- Check permits to work: hot work, lifting, confined space, excavation
- Ensure scaffolds, lifting gear and plant are inspected and certified
- Enforce PPE use
- Investigate and report accidents and near misses (NADOPOD)
- Keep safety records and attend S&H Committee meetings
- Advise the contractor on legal compliance and coordinate emergency response

## Model answers: Part C

### C1. Tipper overturn

**(a) Reportable? Yes.** A fracture is a serious bodily injury, and 14 days' leave exceeds the 4-day threshold (NADOPOD 2004, under OSHA s.32).

- The employer (the earthworks subcontractor) notifies the nearest DOSH office **immediately** by the quickest means.
- Then submits the written report, **JKKP 6**, within **7 days** (online).
- The main contractor, as principal (s.18A), ensures this is done and keeps a copy in the site accident register.

**(b) Possible causes**

| Type | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| Unsafe act | Reversed and tipped too close to the edge |
| Unsafe act | Tipping without a banksman |
| Unsafe condition | Edge softened by heavy rain and not re-inspected |
| Unsafe condition | No earth stop bund or edge marking |
| Management / root cause | No HIRARC or traffic plan for tipping; poor supervision of the subcontractor; end-of-day time pressure |

**(c) Investigation steps**

1. Make the area safe and give first aid; stop tipping at that location.
2. Preserve the scene: barricade it, take photos and measurements.
3. Notify DOSH (see a).
4. Gather evidence: statements from the driver and witnesses; the HIRARC, toolbox talk and inspection records; weather records; vehicle condition.
5. Analyse the root cause, e.g. using 5 Whys or a fishbone diagram.
6. Recommend corrective actions with a PIC and deadline for each.
7. Report to management and the S&H Committee; share lessons in toolbox talks; follow up until all actions are closed.

**(d) Controls and legal responsibility**

- Engineering: earth stop bund at the tipping point; tip back from the edge and push fill over by dozer.
- Administrative: banksman mandatory at tipping; edge inspection by the supervisor after rain, with tipping suspended until it's cleared; update HIRARC and the traffic management plan; toolbox talk to all drivers.
- **Responsibility:** the subcontractor as employer (s.15) and the main contractor as principal (s.18A) for safe systems of work; both must assess risk (s.18B). The driver also has a duty under s.24.

### C2. HIRARC: lifting and pitching precast piles

| Hazard | Consequence | L | S | R | Controls (by hierarchy) | Residual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rig on soft ground near river | Rig overturn, multiple fatalities | 3 | 5 | **15 H** | Engineering: designed and certified working platform, crane mats. Administrative: bearing check, inspect after rain, no work until signed off. | 1×5 = 5 M |
| Suspended pile, sling failure or swing | Struck by pile, death | 3 | 4 | 12 M | Engineering: slings rated and tagged with SWL. Administrative: lifting plan, competent signaller, tag lines, no one under load. | 2×4 = 8 M |
| Pile rolling off stack | Crush injury | 3 | 4 | 12 M | Engineering: chocks/wedges on each tier. Administrative: stack height limit, no access beside stack during lifting. | 2×4 = 8 M |
| Hands between pile and leader | Finger crush, amputation | 3 | 3 | 9 M | Administrative: hands-off guiding with tag lines, pinch-point toolbox talk. PPE: gloves. | 2×3 = 6 M |

**Legal basis:** OSHA **s.18B** requires a risk assessment. s.15(2)(a) requires safe systems of work. The method follows the **DOSH Guidelines for HIRARC 2008**. Because the first hazard is rated High, **work must not start** until the platform controls are in place.
