# HIRARC: Bridge Embankment Earth Filling & Piling

19 Oct 2026

Worked HIRARC example in the DOSH 2008 format, prepared as SSS course practice.

## Scope and assumptions

This HIRARC covers embankment earth filling at a bridge approach and piling for the abutment, on soft ground next to a river. Change the assumptions to match your real site.

| Form header | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Activity | (1) Earth filling for bridge approach embankment; (2) piling for bridge abutment |
| Location | Bridge approach, chainage \_\_\_ (riverside, soft alluvial ground) |
| Conducted by | Site Safety Supervisor, with site engineer and piling subcontractor supervisor |
| Approved by | Project Manager |
| Date / review date | \_\_\_ / review when method, plant or ground conditions change |

**Assumptions:**

- Fill is imported by tipper lorries over a site haul road joining a public road.
- Fill is spread by bulldozer and compacted in layers by a vibratory roller.
- Piles are **precast RC/spun piles driven by a hydraulic hammer rig**. This matches the actual site method.
- Overhead power lines cross near the haul road, and the river is about 10 m from the abutment.

## Rating scales (DOSH HIRARC Guidelines 2008)

Risk = Likelihood (L) × Severity (S). Score 15–25 = **High**, 5–12 = **Medium**, 1–4 = **Low**.

| Score | Likelihood | Severity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | Most likely: the most likely result if the hazard occurs | Catastrophic: many fatalities, major property damage |
| 4 | Possible: has a good chance of occurring | Fatal: approximately one fatality, major injury causing permanent disability |
| 3 | Conceivable: might occur at some time | Serious: non-fatal injury, permanent disability |
| 2 | Remote: has not been known to occur after many years | Minor: disabling but not permanent injury |
| 1 | Inconceivable: practically impossible | Negligible: minor abrasions, bruises, cuts |

**Risk matrix** (H = High, M = Medium, L = Low):

| L \\ S | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **5** | 5 M | 10 M | 15 H | 20 H | 25 H |
| **4** | 4 L | 8 M | 12 M | 16 H | 20 H |
| **3** | 3 L | 6 M | 9 M | 12 M | 15 H |
| **2** | 2 L | 4 L | 6 M | 8 M | 10 M |
| **1** | 1 L | 2 L | 3 L | 4 L | 5 M |

| Rating | Action |
| --- | --- |
| High | Stop or do not start until the risk is reduced. Immediate action and management attention. |
| Medium | Plan and implement controls within a set time. Supervise closely. |
| Low | Acceptable. Maintain existing controls and monitor. |

**Hierarchy of control** (use in this order): elimination → substitution → engineering → administrative → PPE.

Wording varies slightly between course notes, so use NIOSH's version in the exam.

## Work sequence

HIRARC starts by breaking each activity into steps. Each step becomes a row group in the tables below.

**A. Earth filling**

1. Site clearing and topsoil stripping
2. Haulage of fill by tipper lorry (public road and haul road)
3. Tipping at the embankment
4. Spreading by bulldozer and compaction by vibratory roller, layer by layer
5. Field density testing
6. Embankment slope trimming, near the river

**B. Piling**

1. Prepare the working platform and mobilise the piling rig
2. Unload, stack and pitch piles
3. Drive piles with the hydraulic hammer
4. Weld pile joints
5. Cut off pile heads
6. Clean up, demobilise

## A. HIRARC: Earth filling works

The highest risks are plant striking workers and plant overturning at the embankment edge or on soft ground. Ratings are examples. Rate your own site honestly.

## B. HIRARC: Piling works

The single highest risk is **piling rig overturning** on a weak working platform beside the river (rated High). It must be controlled before work starts.

## Legal links and exam tips

In the exam, linking each control to a law turns a site answer into an SSS answer.

| Control in the tables | Legal basis |
| --- | --- |
| Doing the HIRARC at all | OSHA s.18B (risk assessment); DOSH HIRARC Guidelines 2008 |
| Piling subcontractor's safety | OSHA s.18A (principal's duty to contractors) |
| Safe plant, systems of work, training | OSHA s.15 |
| Public road traffic control | OSHA s.17 (persons other than employees) |
| Workers wearing PPE, following rules | OSHA s.24 |
| Rig and crane CF, competent operators | OSHA plant provisions and 2024 regulations (replacing FMA) |
| Lifting, piling, excavation, scaffold or platform rules | BOWEC 1986 (verify current status) |
| Hammer and roller noise | OSH (Noise Exposure) Regs 2019 |
| Reporting an accident | OSHA s.32 + NADOPOD 2004 |

**Tips for writing HIRARC in the exam:**

1. **Hazard ≠ risk ≠ consequence.** Hazard = the source ("suspended pile"). Consequence = what happens ("struck by pile"). Don't write "accident" as a hazard.
2. **Be specific.** Write "tipper reversing at tipping point", not "vehicle movement".
3. **Controls follow the hierarchy.** Start with elimination or engineering. PPE alone is a weak answer.
4. **Residual risk must drop.** Show the new L × S after controls. Severity usually stays the same; likelihood falls.
5. **High risk = stop work.** State clearly that work doesn't start until a High risk is reduced (see B1).
6. **Name a PIC for every control.** Markers check for accountability.

To adapt this for your own site, change the assumptions in Scope and re-rate L and S. Table B assumes driven piles, as on the actual site.
