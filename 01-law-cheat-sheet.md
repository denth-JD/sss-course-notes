# SSS Law Cheat Sheet

NIOSH Site Safety Supervisor course, Kuching, 23 Nov – 4 Dec 2026 · 19 Oct 2026

## The big picture: the law changed on 1 June 2024

On 1 June 2024 Malaysia changed its safety law: OSHA 1994 was amended and the Factories and Machinery Act 1967 was repealed. If your mental model of "the law" is from your early years on site, update it first.

| What | Before 1 June 2024 | From 1 June 2024 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OSHA 1994 (Act 514) | Covered listed industries only | Amended by Act A1648: covers **all places of work** (except armed forces and ships), with much higher penalties |
| FMA 1967 (Act 139) | Machinery, factories, CFs, competent persons | **Repealed** by Act 835. CFs, registrations and competencies issued under FMA now "dealt with under" OSHA (Act 835 s.4) |
| Plant/machinery approval | FMA certificate of fitness (CF) | OSHA plus new 2024 subsidiary legislation on plant requiring a CF |

**How to use this sheet:** it's a revision map, not the law itself. Section numbers marked **(verify)** are from secondary sources or memory, so confirm them against the course notes. Memorise the bold items; skim the rest.

## OSHA 1994 (Act 514), as amended 2022

OSHA is the backbone of the exam. Most essay answers should cite at least one of these sections. **NEW** = added or expanded by the 2022 amendment.

| Section | Topic | What to remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **s.15** | General duties of employers to employees | "So far as is practicable": safe plant and systems of work; safe use, handling, storage and transport of substances; information, instruction, training and supervision; safe workplace, access and exit; safe working environment and welfare |
| **s.15(2)(f)** NEW | Emergency procedures | Employer/principal must develop and implement procedures for emergencies at work |
| **s.16** | Safety and health policy | Written policy, revised as needed, brought to employees' attention (small employers exempted by regulation, verify the threshold) |
| **s.17** | Duties to persons other than employees | Public, visitors, other contractors' workers |
| **s.18** | Duties of occupiers | Safe premises, access and exit for non-employees |
| **s.18A** NEW | Duties of principals | Principal (e.g. main contractor/client) responsible for safety and health of contractors and subcontractors working under their direction |
| **s.18B** NEW | Risk assessment | Employer, self-employed person and principal must carry out risk assessment and implement the controls it shows are needed (this is the legal basis for HIRARC) |
| **s.19** | Penalty for ss.15–18 | Now up to **RM500,000 and/or 2 years** (was RM50,000) (verify exact wording) |
| s.20–23 | Designers, manufacturers, suppliers | Plant and substances safe when properly used; information supplied |
| **s.24** | General duties of employees | Take reasonable care of self and others; cooperate with employer; **wear PPE provided**; comply with instructions |
| s.25 | No interference or misuse | Must not tamper with safety equipment |
| s.26 | No charge to employees | Employer cannot charge workers for anything provided for safety, e.g. PPE |
| **s.26A** NEW | Right to remove oneself from imminent danger | Employee may leave work posing imminent danger, protected from victimisation (know the conditions) |
| s.27 | No discrimination | Employer can't penalise an employee for raising safety complaints or committee work |
| **s.29** | Safety and Health Officer (SHO) | Required for prescribed workplaces. See SHO Order in Regulations below |
| **s.29A** NEW | OSH Coordinator | Employer with **5 or more employees** must appoint one (where no SHO is required, verify) |
| **s.30–31** | Safety and Health Committee | Required with **40 or more** persons employed. s.31 = its functions |
| **s.31A** NEW | Mandatory training | Employer must ensure specified employees attend OSH training |
| **s.32** | Notification of accidents | Notify nearest DOSH office: accidents, dangerous occurrences, occupational poisoning and disease. See NADOPOD |
| s.37 | Industry Codes of Practice | ICOPs approved by the Minister; breaching an ICOP is evidence in proceedings |
| **s.48** | Improvement and prohibition notices | DOSH officer issues: **improvement** = fix by a deadline; **prohibition** = stop the activity now |
| s.49 | Failure to comply with a notice | Offence; penalties increased in 2022 |
| s.50 | Appeal | Against a notice (verify the appeal period) |
| **s.52** | Offences by body corporate | Directors and officers personally liable unless they prove they took reasonable precautions |
| s.66 | Regulations | Minister's power to make regulations, now including ergonomic and psychosocial conditions |

**Exam angle:** know the **duty holders**: employer (s.15), principal (s.18A), occupier (s.18), designer/manufacturer/supplier (s.20–23), employee (s.24). Many questions boil down to "who is responsible and under which section".

## BOWEC 1986 and plant/machinery

BOWEC (Building Operations and Works of Engineering Construction (Safety) Regulations 1986) is the construction-specific rulebook, and **the SSS role comes from it**. It was made under the now-repealed FMA. Ask on Day 1 how NIOSH treats its current status. Expect it to be taught and examined either way.

| Topic | Key points to remember |
| --- | --- |
| **Safety supervisor** | Contractor employing **more than 20 workers** must appoint a safety supervisor (reg. 25, verify) who promotes safe conduct of work on site. This is your future role. |
| Designated person | Competent person appointed to inspect scaffolds, excavations, formwork and lifting gear, and to sign the inspection records |
| **Scaffolds** | Scaffolds **over 40 m high designed by a Professional Engineer** (verify). Inspected by designated person **weekly and after bad weather** (verify). Guardrails, toe boards, sound base plates, ties. Tag system: green = safe, red = do not use. |
| **Excavation** | Deeper than about **1.5 m**: shoring, battering or other support (verify). Barricade the edges. Keep spoil and plant back from the edge. Safe access/egress. Check for underground services first. |
| Formwork and falsework | Designed and erected to drawings, inspected before concreting, struck only when the concrete has enough strength |
| Floor openings and edges | Covered or guarded with guardrails. Covers secured and marked. |
| **Lifting** | Crane must have a valid **certificate of fitness (CF)**. **SWL** marked. Competent operator and signaller. Lifting plan for critical lifts. Lifting gear inspected and tagged. |
| Demolition | Pre-demolition survey, method statement, competent supervision, services disconnected |
| General site | Housekeeping, lighting, first aid, safety helmets and footwear, fire prevention, safe ladders and stairways |

**Plant after the FMA repeal:** plant such as **cranes, passenger lifts, boilers and unfired pressure vessels** still need a CF, now issued under OSHA and its 2024 regulations. CFs and competency certificates issued under FMA carry over (Act 835 s.4). Ask in class for the current CF renewal cycle and the list of plant needing one.

## Key regulations under OSHA

These come up again and again. Learn the **trigger number** for each one: who it applies to, and when.

| Regulation | Trigger / key number | What to remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **OSH (Safety and Health Officer) Order 1997** + SHO Regs 1997 | Building operations with contract value **over RM20 million** need a full-time registered SHO | Below that, a site typically relies on the SSS role. JKR and CIDB contract conditions may require SSO/SSS. |
| **OSH (Safety and Health Committee) Regs 1996** | **40 or more** persons employed (OSHA s.30) | Chair = employer or senior manager; secretary = SHO where there is one; employee reps not fewer than management reps (verify); **meet at least once every 3 months**; inspect the workplace and investigate accidents (verify) |
| **NADOPOD 2004** (Notification of Accident, Dangerous Occurrence, Occupational Poisoning and Occupational Disease) | Death, serious bodily injury, or injury causing **more than 4 calendar days** off work | **Notify DOSH immediately** by the quickest means, then a written report (**JKKP 6**) within **7 days**. Occupational poisoning or disease = **JKKP 7** (verify the form roles). Keep records (verify how long). Submitted online now. |
| **USECHH 2000** (Use and Standards of Exposure of Chemicals Hazardous to Health) | Any chemical hazardous to health | **CHRA** (chemical health risk assessment) by a registered assessor; chemical register; exposure monitoring; medical surveillance; long record retention (verify, about 30 years) |
| **CLASS Regs 2013** | Supplying or using hazardous chemicals | **Safety Data Sheet (SDS)** and **GHS labels**. Know the pictograms. |
| **OSH (Noise Exposure) Regs 2019** | Noisy work: piling, breaking, grinding, compressors | **Action level 82 dB(A)**, **exposure limit 85 dB(A)** (8-hour TWA), **peak 140 dB(C)** (verify). Noise risk assessment, hearing protection, audiometric testing. |

## Other laws and guidelines

These are less likely to be essay questions, but citing them shows breadth.

| Law / guideline | Why it matters on site |
| --- | --- |
| **CIDB Act 1994 (Act 520)** | Construction personnel registration: the **Green Card (CCD)** and its safety induction |
| **CIDB OSHCIM guidelines** (OSH in Construction Industry Management) | Sets out the safety duties of client, designer and contractor across the project life cycle (verify the current edition) |
| **DOSH Guidelines for HIRARC (2008)** | The standard HIRARC format: likelihood × severity matrix and hierarchy of control. This is the format the course will expect. |
| Fire Services Act 1988 | Fire safety, extinguishers, emergency access |
| Employees' Social Security Act 1969 (SOCSO / PERKESO) | Compensation for workplace injury, including for foreign workers (verify the current scheme) |

**Sarawak notes:** OSHA and its regulations are federal law and apply fully in Sarawak. Some related laws differ. Electrical work comes under the **Electricity Ordinance (Sarawak)**, not the Electricity Supply Act 1990. Employment matters come under the **Sarawak Labour Ordinance**, not the Employment Act 1955. Ask the NIOSH Kuching lecturers if these are examined.

## Exam memory aids

**Numbers worth memorising** (confirm each one in class):

| Number | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1 June 2024 | OSHA amendment in force; FMA repealed |
| 5 employees | OSH Coordinator required (s.29A) |
| over 20 workers | BOWEC safety supervisor required |
| 40 persons | Safety and Health Committee required (s.30) |
| RM20 million | Construction contract value needing a full-time SHO |
| RM500,000 / 2 years | Max penalty for employer/principal general duties (s.19) |
| 3 months | S&H Committee meets at least this often |
| more than 4 days | Medical leave that makes an injury reportable (NADOPOD) |
| 7 days | Written JKKP 6 report deadline |
| 1.5 m | Excavation depth needing support |
| 40 m | Scaffold height needing PE design |
| 82 / 85 dB(A) | Noise action level / exposure limit |

**Essay answer structure (L-H-C-R):**

1. **Law:** cite the section or regulation, e.g. "Under OSHA s.15(2)(a)..."
2. **Hazard:** name the specific hazard and its consequence
3. **Control:** follow the hierarchy: elimination → substitution → engineering → administrative → PPE
4. **Responsibility:** who does it, e.g. principal, contractor, SSS, designated person or worker

**Your trap as an experienced engineer:** answering from the "get the job done" angle. The marker wants legal compliance and the hierarchy of control, even when that's less practical on a real site.
